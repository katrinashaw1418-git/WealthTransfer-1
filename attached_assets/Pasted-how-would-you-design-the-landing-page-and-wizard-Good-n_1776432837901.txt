That document is the best structured brief in this entire conversation. My honest opinion section by section:

**What it gets completely right:**

The flow restructure is an improvement over what we built. Moving risk and horizon into a single step 3, then capital into step 4, then recommendation into step 5 is cleaner than the previous 8-step version. It front-loads the profiling work and delivers the reward — the recommendation — earlier. That reduces the moment where users question whether to continue.

The private access block copy is exactly right. "You qualify for private market access" with a gold border and its own section below the recommendation card is the correct treatment. Separating it from the recommendation card entirely makes it feel like an unlock, not an attribute. That is the difference between a feature and a moment.

The reference ID on the confirmation screen with a copy button is non-negotiable and this document calls it correctly. AMX-2026-00482 on screen, large, copyable, before any email arrives. That detail alone reduces support tickets significantly.

The save and resume flow described is the right architecture — auto-save at every step, magic link email if user exits. The document is also right that this is more build than it looks.

The design principles at the bottom — calm not trading UI, outcome-first, progressive disclosure, one action per screen, trust layered everywhere — are the correct five rules for this audience. Print those and put them on the wall before any developer writes a line of code.

**Where I would push back:**

The document describes step 7 as full KYC — personal info, address, source of funds, ID verification — inside the wizard. That is too heavy. Full KYC after account creation is the correct pattern. The wizard should capture name, email, phone, country. Everything else belongs in a post-login onboarding flow where the user is already committed. Putting ID upload in the wizard kills completion rates.

The featured investment section with Islington is correctly identified as the unfair advantage. But it needs one more thing the document does not mention — a minimum investment figure and an eligibility gate. Showing a private deal to someone who selected under $10k in step 4 creates expectation mismatch. The Islington card should only render for $100k+ applicants.

**The overall verdict:**

This document, combined with everything built in this conversation, is a complete institutional product brief. The positioning line at the end — Wealthfront clean, private bank serious, real deals as the edge — is the sharpest one-line summary of what AMAX Wealth should be.
