That document is excellent product thinking and I agree with almost all of it. Let me give you my honest take point by point.

**What it gets exactly right:**

The framing of "collect → interpret → respond" versus "collect → submit → wait" is the sharpest insight in the whole document. That is not just a UX observation — it is the entire product positioning argument. Most wealth platforms are forms with a brand on top. The dynamic recommendation makes this a service. That distinction is worth protecting above everything else.

The three-layer read on what the five fixes actually accomplish — drop-off reduction, regulatory defensibility, and HNW conversion trigger — is senior thinking. Most product teams would call these "UX improvements" and deprioritise them. Framing them as compliance posture and conversion triggers gets them into the sprint.

The private access callout point is pushed further than I did and it is correct. "You've unlocked something" is exactly the emotional signal that converts a $500k+ applicant. It should feel like a velvet rope moment, not a checkbox.

**Where I would push back slightly:**

The save and continue later suggestion is right in principle but the document says "don't overbuild" and then describes a full magic link session restore system with per-step data persistence. That is actually a meaningful backend build. For a first release targeting a small cohort of HNWI applicants, a simpler solution — "email yourself a link to resume" with just the email captured at step 1 — gets 80% of the value at 10% of the cost.

The recommended range "2–4 goals" on the counter is a nice touch but I would validate it first. Some family office investors will select one goal deliberately. Telling them their selection count is below a recommendation range could feel presumptuous for that audience.

**The overall verdict:**

The document is correct that you are past early-stage UX and into institutional product territory. The priority order it gives — recommendation screen first, private access second, AFSL placement third — is the right sequence. Those three alone move the product from "impressive demo" to "credible institutional onboarding."

Want me to rebuild the wizard now with all five upgrades applied — the selection counter, private access callout as a standalone gold-bordered block, application reference number on screen, AFSL disclaimer in three places, and the recommendation card fully enhanced?
